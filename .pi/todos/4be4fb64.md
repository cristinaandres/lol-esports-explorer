Two labels that say something the code does not do, found while collapsing duplicates:

1. **"Back to the eras" goes home, on two not-found pages.** `EventDetail.tsx:54` and `EraDetail.tsx:31` render `<Link to="/">Back to the eras</Link>`. Five other pages use the same phrase and point at `/` only where that is honest (`NotFound`), while `ChampionDetail`, `GameDetail`, `PlayerDetail`, `TeamDetail` and `SeriesDetail` also use `/` — so decide once: either the phrase names the destination and the link goes to `/eras`, or the phrase says where it actually goes ("Back to the home page"). The era and event pages are the mismatches; the others need the same decision.

2. **A singular/plural ternary with identical branches.** `EventDetail.tsx:168` reads `{count(phase.series.length)} {phase.series.length === 1 ? 'series' : 'series'}` — the count and the word already read correctly ("1 series", "4 series"; "series" is invariant), so the ternary is dead weight that reads as a bug and invites a wrong "fix". Delete it, or note in a comment why the noun never changes.

Neither breaks a reader; both are the kind of thing a reviewer spends time on for nothing.

Check: no page says "Back to the eras" while linking somewhere that is not the eras index, and `grep -n "? 'series'" src` finds nothing.

Decided once: the phrase names the destination, so the destination is the eras index. Eight pages said "Back to the eras" and linked `/`; `PhaseDetail` was already right. All nine now point at `/eras`.

Proved in the built site: on `/players/nope`, `/eras/nope`, `/events/nope`, `/champions/nope`, `/games/nope`, `/series/nope`, `/teams/nope` and a 404, the link labelled "Back to the eras" resolves to `/lol-esports-explorer/eras`.

The identical-branch ternary is gone: `series` is invariant, so `count(phase.series.length)` now prints the noun once and the reader sees "4 series" / "1 series" exactly as before.
